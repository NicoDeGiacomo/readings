## Why I read this

Read out of curiosity. When Claude Code came out, I wanted to learn about the person it was named after — Claude Shannon, who founded information theory.

## Key ideas

- **Information became measurable.** Shannon gave it a unit (the bit) and a formula (entropy), turning an intuitive, fuzzy concept into a quantitative science.
- **Meaning is orthogonal to information.** The theory deliberately ignores semantics — a message's information content depends only on its statistical improbability, not on what it's about.
- **Uncertainty *is* information.** The more unpredictable a message, the more information it carries. Perfect predictability carries zero information.
- **There is a floor to compression.** Every message has an entropy — a theoretical minimum length below which it cannot be faithfully represented.
- **There is a ceiling to transmission.** Every channel has a maximum rate (capacity). Below it, near-perfect reliability is achievable; above it, errors are unavoidable.
- **Redundancy is the price of reliability.** Because noise is inevitable, sending more bits than the message strictly contains is the only way to guarantee survival of a signal.

## Personal takeaways

This paper founded information theory — a mathematical framework for quantifying information that has only grown more important with time.
